# P2P Foundation — Transition to Cosmolocal
## Business Plan & 12-Month Budget (€33,000)

*Draft — June 2026*

---

## 1. Who We Are and What We Believe

For twenty years, the P2P Foundation has championed a simple idea: **people working together as peers can produce remarkable things** — without bosses, without gatekeepers, and without locking knowledge away. We believe knowledge belongs in the commons: free for anyone to use, improve, and pass on.

The next chapter of this idea is **cosmolocalism**: *share knowledge globally, produce locally.* Designs, recipes, protocols, and lessons learned travel the world for free; the actual making — food, housing, energy, tools, care — happens in communities, suited to local conditions. The [Cosmolocal Reader](https://wiki.p2pfoundation.net/Cosmo-Local_Reader) (P2P Foundation, 2021) documents more than 40 living examples, from community wind power to open-source farm tools to disaster relief.

Our greatest asset is the **P2P Foundation wiki**: roughly 38,600 articles — the largest curated knowledge commons on peer production anywhere. This plan keeps that commons alive, and puts it to work.

## 2. The Problem and the Opportunity

The knowledge is there, but it sits still. A cooperative in Medellín may have already solved the problem a fab lab in Ghent is wrestling with — and neither knows the other exists. Twenty years of insight is locked in long encyclopedia pages that only the dedicated ever find.

The opportunity: **turn a quiet archive into a living conversation.** With a modest technical refresh under the hood, the wiki can feed a steady stream of accessible, shareable content — and connect peers to peers (P4P) so communities can find each other by what they need and what they know.

## 3. What We Will Do

**1. Tell the stories.** A year-round content pipeline drawing on the wiki: weekly quote posters and visual explainers, a monthly digest newsletter, short thematic series ("How five communities built their own energy"), and cross-posting to the blog and social media. The wiki stops being a place you have to visit and becomes a voice you hear from.

**2. Grow the community.** Paid part-time curators who welcome new contributors, keep quality high, and surface hidden gems. Quarterly open community calls. Simple contributor guides and translation seeds in two more languages. The goal: a new generation of commoners who feel ownership of this knowledge base.

**3. Connect peers to peers.** We will organize the wiki's knowledge around a shared **cosmolocal vocabulary** — consistent ways of describing designs, places, practices, and organizations — so people can ask practical questions like *"who is working on community energy near me?"* and get answers. This is what makes peer-for-peer (P4P) collaboration real rather than aspirational.

**4. Keep the foundation solid.** None of the above works if the wiki is down, slow, or insecure. We maintain the servers, keep the software current, improve search (including AI-assisted search across all 38,600 articles), and publish complete public backups so this commons can never be lost or captured.

The order matters: the technical work is the *enabler*, not the destination. Every euro spent under the hood exists to make the content, community, and connection work possible.

## 4. Budget — €33,000 / 12 months

### 4.1 Content Production & Dissemination — €12,500 (38%)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Content producer stipend: weekly visual content (quote posters, explainers), monthly digest newsletter, thematic story series (~€700/mo) | €8,400 |
| Social media presence & cross-posting: blog, newsletter platform, federated and mainstream social channels (~€250/mo) | €3,000 |
| Design assets, templates, and a refreshed visual identity for wiki-derived content | €1,100 |

### 4.2 Community Building — €5,000 (15%)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Community curator stipends: welcoming and mentoring new contributors, curating quality (~€300/mo) | €3,600 |
| Quarterly open community calls + contributor documentation | €800 |
| Translation seeds for 2 priority languages | €600 |

### 4.3 Technical Foundations (one-time, enables all of the above) — €9,500 (29%)

| Item | Cost |
|---|---|
| Software refresh: bring the wiki to the current long-term-support version, with modern fast search | €2,300 |
| Shared cosmolocal vocabulary: 2 community workshops to define it, plus the structured-data layer that powers P4P discovery | €4,500 |
| AI-assisted search across the full 38,600-article corpus | €1,500 |
| Monitoring, alerting, and verified public backups | €1,200 |

### 4.4 Keeping the Lights On (recurring) — €4,800 (14.5%)

| Item | Monthly | Annual |
|---|---|---|
| Hosting, domain, off-site backup storage | €40 | €480 |
| Maintenance retainer: security updates, software patches, uptime monitoring (~6 h/mo) | €360 | €4,320 |

### 4.5 Contingency — €1,200 (3.5%)

Reserve for surprises: traffic spikes, urgent security fixes, hardware replacement.

### Budget Summary

| Category | Amount | % |
|---|---|---|
| Content production & dissemination | €12,500 | 38% |
| Community building | €5,000 | 15% |
| Technical foundations (one-time) | €9,500 | 29% |
| Keeping the lights on | €4,800 | 14.5% |
| Contingency | €1,200 | 3.5% |
| **Total** | **€33,000** | **100%** |

**More than half the budget (53%) goes directly to people: producing content, telling stories, and building community.** The technical spend is front-loaded, one-time, and exists to multiply what those people can do.

## 5. Timeline

| Quarter | Milestones |
|---|---|
| **Q1** | Content pipeline launches (posters + newsletter); software refresh; first vocabulary workshop |
| **Q2** | Curators onboarded; first community call; structured vocabulary live on priority topics |
| **Q3** | AI search public beta; first P4P discovery features ("find peers working on…"); first thematic story series complete |
| **Q4** | 3,000+ articles connected to the cosmolocal vocabulary; year-2 sustainability plan (memberships, grants, partnerships) |

## 6. What Success Looks Like

- **Reach:** 500+ newsletter subscribers; 48+ pieces of shareable content published; growing referral traffic to the wiki.
- **Community:** 20+ new active contributors; four well-attended community calls.
- **Connection:** people can search the commons by need and place; at least 3 outside projects building on our open data — proof that P4P collaboration works.
- **Reliability:** the wiki stays up (99.5%+), and anyone in the world can download a complete copy at any time.

## 7. Why €33,000 Goes So Far

The expensive parts already exist: twenty years of curated knowledge, working infrastructure, and a global network that trusts the P2P Foundation name. This budget doesn't build something new from scratch — it activates what is already there. That is the cosmolocal principle applied to ourselves: the knowledge is global and free; the operation is local, lean, and accountable to its community.
